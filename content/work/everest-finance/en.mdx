## Context

Everest Finance is a Senegalese SGI serving retail clients in the UEMOA zone. I joined as the sole technical operator, reporting directly to the CEO, with a clear mandate: stop the drift between disconnected digital surfaces and ship production software the operations team can actually run.

There is no engineering team, no inherited platform, and no dedicated DevOps function. The constraint is one person, multiple products, a CEO who needs velocity, and a regulatory environment that does not tolerate sloppiness with financial data.

The work spans **three products**:

| Product | Where it lives |
|---------|----------------|
| Public website + editorial CMS | [dev-everest-new.vercel.app](https://dev-everest-new.vercel.app/) |
| Sama Naffa — customer savings app + admin | [dev.samanaffa.com](https://dev.samanaffa.com/) |
| Formos — internal campaign capture | Demo only (not yet deployed) |

Digital account opening is linked from public CTAs as a separate deployment — not counted as a fourth product; the CMS and corporate site ship as one.

## Problem

When I arrived, Everest's digital presence was fragmented in ways that created real operational pain:

- The **public website** was a static marketing shell. It could not publish market commentary, capture qualified leads with structure, or route prospects into product flows.
- **Operations** relied on spreadsheets and ad-hoc tools. No single view of onboarding status, no structured lead pipeline for campaigns, no audit trail tied to digital intake.
- **Sama Naffa** existed as a partial implementation spanning legacy product lines (APE and PEE). The business needed a focused savings product — accounts, KYC, deposits — not three half-overlapping product lines.

The deeper problem was independent evolution: separate mental models, separate data stores, no shared conventions. Every new feature risked inconsistency between what the website promised and what clients experienced in the app.

For a regulated financial institution, that drift is operational risk — not technical debt.

## Approach

I did not force a premature monolith. Each surface ships on the stack that matches its risk profile:

- **Right-sized backend per surface.** The public site uses **Convex** for real-time CMS, publications, media metadata, and investor-profiler leads — fast iteration for marketing without running Postgres ops for editorial content. Sama Naffa and Formos use **Neon Postgres + Drizzle** where relational integrity, KYC state, and transaction intents matter.
- **TypeScript everywhere, frameworks chosen per job.** The public site runs on Vite + TanStack Router. Sama Naffa on Next.js 16 App Router. Formos on TanStack Start with oRPC. Same language and discipline; different runtimes where they fit.
- **Incremental delivery by leverage.** Public site and CMS first (visible progress, publications workflow). Sama Naffa ops stack next (real money paths). Formos for internal campaign capture without blocking on the product roadmap.
- **Clear product scope.** Sama Naffa épargne is the only active operations line. Legacy APE and PEE routes remain for historical data but receive no new engineering investment.
- **Quality gates as contract.** Drizzle schemas, Zod validation at API boundaries, automated typecheck and lint across every codebase.

The tradeoff: more surfaces to hold in one head. The win: each ships correctly instead of bending everything into one framework.

## What shipped

**Public website + CMS (live):**

- Full corporate site: services, expertises, capital markets, publications, FAQ, contact
- Editorial CMS: publications with categories, rich text, media on R2, role-based admin
- Inline content editing for marketing copy without redeploys
- **Everest Profiler**: investor questionnaire → lead capture → automated Resend email with profile report
- CTAs routing to digital account opening and Sama Naffa journeys

**Sama Naffa — savings app (production):**

- Client onboarding (phone OTP, multi-step registration, post-login profile completion)
- KYC via **Didit** with portal resume and admin exception handling
- **SAMA_NAFFA** account creation and client portal (balances, deposit intents)
- Deposits through **Intouch** with PENDING → PROCESSING → COMPLETED intent tracking
- Admin back-office: users, transactions, KYC status, notification settings, balance recalculation

**Formos — internal lead capture (demo):**

- Admin-authenticated form builder with versioned JSON definitions
- Public multi-step forms at `/f/:slug`
- Lead pipeline in `/admin/leads`
- Submission events to **Inngest** for async processing

**Cross-cutting:**

- Independent Vercel and Convex Cloud deployments per surface
- Drizzle migrations and schema-as-contract on Postgres surfaces
- Mobile web first for the savings product (no native app in scope)

## Technical depth

The hardest problem is holding operational truth across surfaces that will never share one database.

On the public site, Convex gives real-time CMS mutations and scheduled actions (profiler follow-up emails) without standing up API infrastructure for editorial workflows. Investor profiler leads are first-class records with audit fields, not a Mailchimp export.

On Sama Naffa, the database schema is the contract: users, accounts, deposit intents, KYC enums, notification settings. Transaction state machines live server-side; the admin UI renders state and allowed transitions — it does not invent business rules. Rate limiting, session versioning, and phone normalization exist because production ops surfaced real edge cases.

Formos solves a different problem: marketing and compliance need campaign-specific intake without engineering a new form page every week. Versioned form definitions in Postgres, publish workflow, and background jobs on submission keep intake structured without coupling to the savings app schema.

## Outcome

As of mid-2026:

- **Public website:** Live. Publications workflow in use. Profiler generating qualified leads with automated follow-up.
- **Sama Naffa:** Active ops on savings — onboarding, KYC, deposits, admin back-office. Hardening edge cases on transaction flows before wider marketing push.
- **Formos:** Internal tool for structured campaign capture and lead review.
- **Account opening:** Linked from public site; separate deployment on the consolidation roadmap.

This is not a finished unified platform. It is three products with honest boundaries between marketing data (Convex), client money data (Neon), and internal intake (Formos).

## Lessons

**What I'd do differently:**

- **Define active product scope on day one.** Sama Naffa carried legacy APE/PEE scope that diluted engineering for months. A clear scope document would have saved significant rework.
- **Set accurate expectations with stakeholders early.** "One unified platform" sounds reassuring; "three products, one operator" sets honest expectations about velocity and risk.
- **More behavioral tests on money paths.** TypeScript and Drizzle catch structural drift; they do not catch a deposit intent stuck between statuses.

**What worked:**

- Matching backend to surface risk (Convex for CMS, Postgres for money) avoided over-engineering the marketing stack.
- Shipping the visible public site first kept stakeholder confidence while the harder Sama Naffa ops work continued underneath.
- Formos as a small internal product removed "can we spin up a campaign form this week?" from the critical path.
